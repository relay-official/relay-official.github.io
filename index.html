RELAY: Accelerating Online VLA Reinforcement Learning through Adaptive Semantic Reuse

Anonymous Authors

Abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies owe much of their semantic competence to pretrained vision-language backbones. In online reinforcement learning, however, the same backbone imposes a recurring computational cost: repeated semantic inference occupies the action-generation critical path and throttles experience collection. We present RELAY, which decouples the two processes by reusing cached semantic representations across nearby decisions and refreshing them as the scene changes. A lightweight temporal adapter aligns cached semantics with the current execution context, allowing the action policy to maintain high-frequency closed-loop control while semantic inference proceeds asynchronously. In simulation, RELAY delivers 1.70× the rollout throughput of the synchronous baseline and reduces complete online-RL time by 26.5%, while maintaining final task success comparable to the evaluated baselines. Under a fixed two-hour real-robot learning budget, this efficiency translates into 87.5% terminal success across four manipulation tasks, 18.75 percentage points above the strongest evaluated baseline.
